Product description:

Murmuration is an intelligent neighbourhood energy coordination platform that prevents electricity demand rebound peaks by coordinating flexible appliance schedules across multiple households.

Independent electricity optimizers can make thousands of households shift consumption to the same cheap tariff period. This creates a new demand spike and can overload local transformers.

Murmuration solves this coordination problem by simulating neighbourhood demand, scheduling flexible household loads around shared transformer capacities, preserving household constraints, and measuring the resulting financial and environmental impact.

FlockShield is the capacity-aware coordination and validation engine. Murmuration GridLab is the interactive digital twin used to demonstrate its effectiveness.

The central innovation is coordinated energy optimization across households, not another individual electricity bill calculator.

# 4. THREE COMPARISON STRATEGIES

Implement three genuinely different strategies using the same synthetic population and input conditions.

## Strategy A: Unmanaged baseline

- Use the household's original appliance start times.
- Respect each appliance's physical run duration.
- Calculate total demand at each time slot.
- Report transformer overloads when the original demand exceeds capacity.

## Strategy B: Independent optimization

- Optimize each household without knowing its neighbours' schedules.
- Use the original independent optimizer or a corrected equivalent.
- Allow many homes to select the same cheap tariff period.
- Demonstrate how independently optimized demand can create a rebound peak.

Do not artificially force this strategy to fail. Configure a scenario in which synchronized starts naturally emerge from the tariff and appliance windows.

## Strategy C: FlockShield coordination

- Coordinate appliance scheduling across households.
- Consider shared transformer capacity when assigning time slots.
- Prefer cheaper and cleaner periods when feasible.
- Spread flexible loads across available capacity instead of synchronizing them.
- Respect household load limits, appliance deadlines, and comfort constraints.
- Recalculate costs and carbon emissions from the final schedule.

Use a deterministic, capacity-aware heuristic suitable for the hackathon timeline. A practical implementation can use congestion penalties, ranked scheduling, candidate time-slot evaluation, and local rescheduling.

You do not need a complex distributed consensus protocol for the MVP. Implement the coordinated behavior correctly first and keep the coordination engine isolated behind a clean interface.

Never claim global optimality unless it has actually been established.

# 5. FLOCKSHIELD SAFETY ENGINE

Implement FlockShield as a separate, testable module.

Each home belongs to a transformer. Each transformer has a configured usable capacity in kW.

For every transformer and every simulation time slot, verify:

sum of the aggregate demand of all connected homes <= transformer capacity

Also enforce:

- Each household's sanctioned-load limit.
- Appliance start windows and completion deadlines.
- Correct appliance run durations.
- Non-overlapping or overlapping appliance behavior as explicitly permitted by the profile and load limits.
- Geyser temperature constraints.
- AC comfort limits.
- Existing fixed loads that cannot be shifted.

For the initial MVP, use 48 half-hour slots per day.

Treat power ratings in kW, slot durations in hours, energy in kWh, tariffs in INR/kWh, and emissions in kg CO2/kWh.

Do not confuse power with energy.

A transformer capacity check must include all connected homes and all active appliances in each slot. Checking each home independently is insufficient.

Add a final schedule validator after coordination.

If a valid schedule cannot be found, report the infeasibility and identify the conflicting constraints. Do not silently claim safety.

Important physical limitation: software scheduling cannot guarantee the safety of a real electrical grid in every circumstance. The project demonstrates simulated, capacity-constrained scheduling, not certified grid protection.
